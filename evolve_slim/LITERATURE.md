# Learning sparse linear models with integer coefficients: literature notes

A scoring system (risk score, point system) is a linear model `score = sum_j lambda_j x_j` with few nonzero coefficients, each a small integer (typically in {-5..5}). A prediction is a threshold on the score (SLIM) or a logistic map of it, `P(y=1) = 1/(1+exp(-(lambda_0 + score)/m))` (RiskSLIM, FasterRisk). Work falls into three groups: (1) exact mixed-integer programming (MIP) formulations that can certify optimality but need a commercial solver and scale to tens of features; (2) heuristics that fit a sparse continuous model and then round it (FasterRisk, AutoScore, rounding methods); (3) generic L0 best-subset solvers (L0Learn, abess, fastSparse, OKRidge) that produce the sparse continuous model a rounding step needs. All entries below were checked against the arXiv/publisher page and the code repository in September 2026.

## Notes

- **What certifies.** Only the MIP/BnB family (SLIM, RiskSLIM, MISS, Rouzot et al., DILSVM, bAUC, Molero-Río & D'Ambrosio) returns an optimality gap for the integer problem. OKRidge and Liu-Shafiee-Lodi certify the continuous k-sparse problem, not the integer one. FasterRisk, GroupFasterRisk, AutoScore and PSL are heuristics.
- **Commercial solver dependency.** SLIM, RiskSLIM and MISS are tied to CPLEX. No maintained open-source-solver port of RiskSLIM exists in the official repos as of this check; the RiskSLIM README lists python-mip support as planned. RiskSLIM MINLP can be rewritten for SCIP or HiGHS with lazy cutting-plane callbacks, but that is not packaged.
- **FasterRisk vs RiskSLIM.** In the FasterRisk paper RiskSLIM (CPLEX 12.8, 15-minute limit, also rerun with 1 hour) hits the time limit on most model sizes for adult, bank, mushroom, COMPAS, FICO, spambase and Netherlands, and solves only the small cases (mammo, breastcancer). FasterRisk finishes in under about 100 s per model size and matches or beats RiskSLIM's training loss and test AUC. Other baselines there: ElasticNet pools (1,100 glmnet models) followed by simple rounding, unit weighting, rescaled rounding, randomized rounding, sequential rounding; AutoScore; OMP and fastSparse followed by sequential rounding.
- **Two-stage recipe.** Most fast methods are "sparse continuous solution, then round". Any of fastSparse, L0Learn, abess, or FasterRisk's own SparseBeamLR can supply stage 1; FasterRisk's star-ray multiplier search and sequential rounding are the strongest published stage 2.
- **Direct improvements on FasterRisk (2023-2026).** GroupFasterRisk (group sparsity, monotonicity) and RegScore (regression) extend it; SHAPoint, Gradient Boosted Risk Scores and AgentScore change the model class or the search (Shapley-derived points, boosting, LLM-proposed rules) and report gains over FasterRisk or AutoScore on their own benchmarks. No 2023-2026 paper was found that reports a clear, reproduced improvement over FasterRisk on the standard RiskSLIM/FasterRisk binary benchmarks under the same k and coefficient bounds.
- **Related surveys.** "Responsible Machine Learning via Mixed-Integer Optimization" ([arXiv 2505.05857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05857)) and "Trustworthy Machine Learning through the Lens of Combinatorial Optimization" ([arXiv 2607.07762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07762)) cover scoring systems among other MIO models.

**Where to download binarized versions.**
- [ustunb/risk-slim `examples/data`](https://github.com/ustunb/risk-slim/tree/master/examples/data): `adult_data.csv`, `bank_data.csv`, `breastcancer_data.csv` (+ `_cvindices.csv`, `_weights.csv`), `mammo_data.csv`, `mushroom_data.csv`, `spambase_data.csv`. First column is the label in {-1, +1}, remaining columns are binarized features.
- FasterRisk's [example notebook](https://github.com/jiachangliu/FasterRisk/blob/main/docs/example.ipynb) downloads a fixed adult train/test split from Google Drive with `fasterrisk.utils.download_file_from_google_drive` (file ids `1nuWn0QVG8tk3AN4I4f3abWLcFEP3WPec` train, `1TyBO02LiGfHbatPWU4nzc8AndtIF-7WH` test) and a raw Pima diabetes file (`184JhmJiSEUiBCo8ySAD8adDn_S9rjmjM`) for its binarization demo. The FasterRisk repo has no `data/` folder.
- COMPAS, FICO and Netherlands are binarized in FasterRisk with threshold dummies `1[x_j <= theta]` for every unique value theta (1,000 quantiles for two Netherlands age features), following Sec. C2 of the fastSparse paper; the processed files are not published, so they must be rebuilt from the raw sources.
- SLIM's processed datasets are in the paper's Online Resource 1 (Springer supplementary material).
